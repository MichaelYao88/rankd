How to play

One new puzzle every day. Here's how each mode works.

The daily puzzle

A new puzzle appears every day at midnight in your local time, and everyone plays the same one. The card at the top tells you the mode (Rank it, Top 5, Tier it, Match it or Name it), gives the puzzle a headline, and says exactly what is being measured, for example "Average annual hours worked per worker" or "Arrests per 100,000 people". Read that line carefully: the measure, the unit and the year often decide the answer.

Rank it

You get five or six cards, each a country or a group, and the real values in a column beside them (highest at the top — a few puzzles are flipped and say so). Match each card to the right number. Drag the cards, or tap the ▲ and ▼ arrows, then press the white Lock it in button.

After each lock-in, cards in the right spot turn green and are pinned in place. Cards in the wrong spot turn red and shake. Rearrange the red ones and lock in again. You have five tries. Solve it in one or two tries for a celebration. Miss every card on a country or demographic Rank it and you may see a cheeky Racist! or Xenophobe! flash. If you run out of tries, the correct order is revealed with every value, each item's rank among all countries in the source, and a short fact about the result.

Top 5

You get six country cards: the real top 5, plus one famous country that people expect to be up there but actually ranks 6th or lower. The five values sit in a column. Drag the cards into slots #1 through #5, highest first, or tap a card and then a slot. Leave the odd one out.

Press Lock it in. Slots you got right turn green and stay put. Wrong slots turn red. Move those and lock in again. You have five tries. Each missed lock reveals one hint about the highest-ranked slot you still have wrong. When the answer comes up, the card you were supposed to leave out shows its real rank and value, for example “Not top 5: Ireland is actually #10”. If two countries tie for fifth, either one can fill that slot.

Tier it

Tier it is for stats where several countries share the same value. Drop each country onto a labeled tier row (several can share a tier). Drag a chip onto a row, or tap a chip and then the row. Lock it in: right tiers turn green and lock, wrong ones turn red. You get five tries. After you finish, the real percentage stays on each chip.

Name it

Name it asks for any country that satisfies two rules at once: a letter or word rule, and a stat rule. One example is a country starting with S whose Human Development Index is above the world average. Type a country and pick it from the list (USA, Korea and the other usual aliases work). Any valid country wins. You get five tries. A guess that isn't a country, or a country you already tried, doesn't use a try. Each wrong guess gets one short line saying which rule it missed, or both. When you finish, every valid answer is shown as a flag chip, sorted by the stat, with your pick highlighted and the share of players who named that same country.

Match it

Match it shows four to seven people or countries and the same number of clues. Each clue is a true, sourced fact about exactly one of them. Drag a clue onto a person, or tap a clue and then tap the person. When every clue is placed, press Lock it in. Right pairs turn green and lock, wrong pairs turn red, and you fix them and try again, up to five tries. The answer screen lists every pair with the fuller story and a link to its source.

Values, sources and caveats

Values are always shown: Rankd is about knowing which number belongs to which country, not about memorising the numbers. Every puzzle names its source and links to it on the answer screen, along with the year of the data. Some puzzles deal with sensitive subjects like crime, health, religion or politics. These carry a short caveat explaining what the numbers measure and what they don't (for example, that arrests are not convictions, or that a survey was self-reported). Puzzles about suicide include a link to crisis support.

Practice

Practice deals a random puzzle from the full datasets behind the game, so you can keep playing after the daily. Each Practice puzzle is built on the spot from a real ranked list with the same rules as the daily puzzles: items are well spread out, so no two values are too close to call, and well-known countries are mixed in. Press 🎲 New for another one. Practice games don't count toward your stats or streak.

Archive and streaks

Missed a day? Every past daily puzzle is in the Archive, with a tick for the ones you've played. Archive games are just for fun and don't change your streak. Your streak counts the days in a row on which you played the daily puzzle on that day. Your stats (games played, solved, current and best streak) and your progress are saved only in your browser on this device. Nothing is sent to us. You can wipe it all at any time in Settings → Your data → Clear all data.

Sharing

When you finish, press Share my result to copy a spoiler-free summary (coloured squares, no answers) with a link, so friends can play the same puzzle.
