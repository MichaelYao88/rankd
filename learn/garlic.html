Who eats the most garlic per person?

This ranki.gg Rank it uses one measure: FAO apparent consumption per person, in kg/yr per capita. The countries on the card are China, South Korea, India, Brazil, Spain, Italy. You do not have to recall the numbers from memory. They sit in a column. The job is to match each country to the value that belongs to it, then lock the order in. Five tries. A card in the right spot locks. A card in the wrong spot turns red so you can move it.

Italy eats little garlic: heavy use is mainly southern, not national. About four in five Italian immigrants to the US, 1880-1920, came from the south and Sicily, so Italian-American food leans on it. Note: FAO supply, not food eaten.

China leads this table at 13.73 kg/yr per capita. After that: South Korea at 6.2 (7.53 kg/yr per capita under China); India at 2.28 (3.92 kg/yr per capita under South Korea); Brazil at 1.42 (0.86 kg/yr per capita under India); Spain at 1.1 (0.32 kg/yr per capita under Brazil); Italy at 0.83 (0.27 kg/yr per capita under Spain). China is 16.542 times Italy. That multiple is the top row divided by the bottom row of this same table. In the wider list of 182 stored on the puzzle, China is number 1, South Korea is number 4, India is number 35, Brazil is number 54, Spain is number 66, Italy is number 85.

The fact on the puzzle is the reason Italy sits at the bottom of a card people expect it to lead. Heavy garlic use is mainly southern, not a national average, and the fact ties Italian-American cooking to the immigrants who came from the south and Sicily between 1880 and 1920. The source line says the kilograms are production plus imports minus exports, divided by population, for 2023, computed for ranki.gg from FAOSTAT. The fact's own note says this is supply, not food eaten.

The source line on the puzzle is: FAOSTAT (FAO): Crops and livestock products (garlic, item 406), Trade (import/export quantity) and Population, 2023; per-person figures computed by ranki.gg. The puzzle does not add a separate caveat beyond that source line and the fact above. ranki.gg does not invent a value that is missing from that source. If two countries were so close that the order would depend on rounding, they would not be the pair the card asks you to separate.

When you finish the puzzle, the answer screen repeats the order, the fact, and a link to the source. That link is the same URL cited here: https://www.fao.org/faostat/en/#data/QCL.
