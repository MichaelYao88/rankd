Most speakers worldwide

This ranki.gg Rank it uses one measure: Total speakers, native plus second language (L1+L2), in million. The countries on the card are English, Mandarin, Hindi, Spanish, Standard Arabic, French. You do not have to recall the numbers from memory. They sit in a column. The job is to match each country to the value that belongs to it, then lock the order in. Five tries. A card in the right spot locks. A card in the wrong spot turns red so you can move it.

English leads only through second languages: Mandarin has far more native speakers. Standard Arabic has almost no native speakers, because children learn a local dialect first, and it still edges French.

English leads this table at 1,493 million. After that: Mandarin at 1,183 (310 million under English); Hindi at 611 (572 million under Mandarin); Spanish at 561 (50 million under Hindi); Standard Arabic at 335 (226 million under Spanish); French at 334 (1 million under Standard Arabic). English is 4.47 times French. That multiple is the top row divided by the bottom row of this same table. In the wider list of 200 stored on the puzzle, English is number 1, Mandarin is number 2, Hindi is number 3, Spanish is number 4, Standard Arabic is number 5, French is number 6.

The fact says English leads only through second languages, because Mandarin has far more native speakers, and that Standard Arabic has almost no native speakers because children learn a local dialect first, yet Standard Arabic still edges French. Hindi and Spanish are the middle of this six. The counts are total speakers, native plus second language, from the Ethnologue 29th edition as cited on the Wikipedia list the source line names.

The source line on the puzzle is: Ethnologue 29th edition (2026), total speakers (L1+L2). Wikipedia: List of languages by total number of speakers, Ethnologue (2026) section (oldid 1380301586), citing Ethnologue's top-200 list. The puzzle does not add a separate caveat beyond that source line and the fact above. ranki.gg does not invent a value that is missing from that source. If two countries were so close that the order would depend on rounding, they would not be the pair the card asks you to separate.

When you finish the puzzle, the answer screen repeats the order, the fact, and a link to the source. That link is the same URL cited here: https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=List_of_languages_by_total_number_of_speakers&oldid=1380301586.
